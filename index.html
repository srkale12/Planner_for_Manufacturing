Manufacturing Planner

How the data flows

A production planning system, drawn end to end: where the numbers come from, where the decision actually gets made, and where each result lands.

A factory makes hundreds of products on a handful of production lines. Every week somebody has to decide what to make, on which line, on which day, and how much — balancing three things that pull against each other: filling customer orders, not freezing cash in inventory, and not losing hours to washing lines between products.

This is the system that makes that decision. Everything narrows into one place and fans back out: eight data sources enter at the top, get squeezed through a single engine, and come out as six sets of results spread across the screens a planner clicks. The shape is an hourglass, and the waist is where all the value is.

Reading the diagram

The three zones

Top — where data comes from

Eight tables, all living in one database, all tagged with a scenario label. That label is what lets two customers — or one planner running what-ifs — share the same system without ever seeing each other's numbers.

Middle — where the decision is made

The only place anything is actually decided. Four steps: set the quantities, spread them across days, make the result physically realistic, then use any capacity left over. Everything above it is preparation; everything below it is presentation.

Bottom — where output is shown

Six sets of results fan out to five screens plus the KPI bar. One of them — the audit snapshot — doesn't go to a screen at all. It goes to the ERP as a proposal, and to the record that proves what was recommended and by whom.

About

What this is

The system
A production planning system for batch manufacturing. It builds a twelve-week plan across multiple lines, balancing order fulfilment, inventory investment, and changeover time.
What it weighs
Four goals that pull against each other: filling customer orders on time, holding enough safety stock, not tying up cash in excess inventory, and using shifts efficiently. It also sequences production day by day so compatible products run together and lines spend less time being washed out.
Built with
Python, FastAPI, React, and SQL. Ships three ways from one codebase: a hosted web app, a Windows desktop installer, and an ERP connector.
By design
It recommends; it never writes to a system of record. A planner reviews every proposal, the ERP creates the actual production orders, and every recommendation is logged with who ran it and what it said.
